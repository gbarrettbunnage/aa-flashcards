Introduction

It is important to understand that in Advanced Assurance, you will be required to apply your knowledge to the information given in the scenario.



Active reading of the scenario and critical evaluation of each piece of information given in the question are the main techniques that need to be applied while practising these questions.



The key techniques to remember are:



For audit sampling questions (where results from testing are provided):



1

Identify nature and causes of misstatement 



2

Extrapolate and project the misstatement to the population (where appropriate) 



3

Evaluate the results



4

Conclude or determine further actions/procedures.



For performing substantive procedures questions:



1

Perform a high-level review of the question including the requirement and appendices 



2

Identify if you are required to design substantive procedures as well as perform them 



3

Perform the substantive procedures and identify any issues requiring further investigation



4

Document your findings including any conclusions or follow-up procedures required



Remember, you will not be provided with a substantive testing programme and you will need to assess which procedures are possible and appropriate based on the information provided.



For outstanding matters questions: 



1

Discuss the matter and why it is relevant to the audit before listing further procedures to gather the additional evidence required.    



2

Look out for information in the scenario, such as materiality, to help you determine whether the matter is material and would impact the financial statements. 



For audit misstatements questions:



1

Is the misstatement material Yes/No or could it be material by nature?



2

If multiple misstatements occur in the same balance/transaction/disclosure, are they material in aggregate?



3

Does the item require correction now or could it require correction in due course?



4

Does the auditor need to perform any further procedures, including discussing the issue with management, to help reach a final conclusion?



5

Have you been asked to prepare a summary of audit misstatements (SAM)? If so, use the example format provided to you in this module

Solution

The following section presents an example of how you should structure your answer. 



(a) Outstanding point from audit



(b) Additional actions or evidence required



Haverton Council 



The revenue of £199,000 has been agreed to the order. However, the revenue appears to have been recognised as income upon receipt of the order and payment rather than on delivery of the goods to Haverton Council.



&#x20;



As such, revenue may be materially overstated in the year to 30 April 20X6 by £199,000.



&#x20;



Inspect the bank statement to agree the receipt of £199,000 at 29 March 20X6 and enquire with the production manager regarding the status of the order at 30 April 20X6 to determine whether the items had been produced or despatched by the year-end.



&#x20;



If the play equipment had been dispatched by 30 April 20X6, inspect the goods dispatched note (GDN), vouching that the date on the GDN is pre-year-end and that the delivery note was signed by a responsible official from Haverton Council to confirm delivery of the play equipment.



&#x20;



If the play equipment had not been dispatched by 30 April 20X6, then this is a material misstatement and an adjustment is required to the financial statements. The error should be reported to those charged with governance in our summary of audit misstatements (SAM).



Maintenance contract 



&#x20;



Although the maintenance costs incurred during the year have been agreed with no issues detected, it is noted that a potentially related party transaction exists as Clean N Easy Ltd’s CEO is a close family member of Kingdom’s finance director, as well as Sam Willis being the majority shareholder in Clean N Easy Ltd. 



&#x20;



There is a risk that the related party transaction has not been adequately disclosed in the financial statements.



&#x20;



&#x20;



Enquire of Sam Willis regarding his relationship to Clean N Easy Ltd and any other interests in entities that may be considered related parties, including any related party transactions.



&#x20;



Confirm the total value of the transactions during 20X5-X6 and ensure that this, and any outstanding balances, are disclosed appropriately in the financial statements.



&#x20;



If the relevant disclosures have not been included or are incorrect, then we should request that an adjustment is made to correct the related party disclosure note in the financial statements. 



Insurance costs: 



The insurance costs have been agreed to the invoice. However, £36,000 of the amount due relates to 20X6-20X7 and should be recognised as a prepayment as at 30 April 20X6.



&#x20;



Therefore, there is a risk that insurance costs are overstated. However, this is below materiality and, therefore, does not have a material impact on the financial statements.



&#x20;



Inspect a breakdown of prepayments to confirm if a relevant prepayment of £36,000 has been recognised for this transaction as at 30 April 20X6.  



&#x20;



If no prepayment has been recognised, then this should be reported to those charged with governance within our summary of audit misstatements, as it is below materiality but above the trivial threshold. 



#### Question 2



scenario



Read the following scenario.  



&#x20;



It is June 20X4. You are the audit senior in charge of Holly Farm Ltd (‘Holly’), a large farming audited entity of your firm, for the year ended 31 December 20X3. The audit fieldwork was completed in early May, the file has been reviewed and you are at the stage of confirming any events after the reporting period prior to final completion. The final draft of the financial statements is being prepared and you will receive them for review later this week. The financial statements are due to be signed off at a board meeting scheduled for next week.  



Holly grows many cereal crops as well as having dairy farming units. These also form a substantial portion of the turnover. Holly is a family-owned company, the original small farm known as ‘Holly Farm’ having been worked by the family for five generations. During the last 20 years, the business has grown by buying neighbouring farms and Holly is now one of the largest farming operations in Dorset, England. 



Holly farms organically and has developed and cultivated relationships with local businesses. In addition to wholesale customers, Holly sells directly to local businesses such as farm shops, creameries and bakers. The directors consider the local origin and supply of the organic produce from Holly to be a unique selling point and are very proud of the fact that the produce is high quality and ultimately customers can trace the origin back to Holly. 



Two years ago, the company expanded again, this time diversifying by starting up a trout fish farming division in the North of Scotland.  



In order to complete the audit, your senior manager has asked you to design audit procedures to resolve the outstanding points arising from the audit. You have reviewed the audit file and extracted matters outstanding (Appendix).  



Your senior manager would like you to consider any potential adjustments arising from the outstanding matters. Therefore, your senior manager would like you to draft a summary of audit misstatements (‘SAM’) with conclusions justifying which items require adjustment.  



&#x20;



Question: 



Design audit procedures to gather audit evidence to resolve the matters outstanding noted in the Appendix.



&#x20;



Prepare a SAM noting, where applicable, any potential adjustments arising from the outstanding matters noted and conclude as to which items may require adjustment.



Appendix



Revenue for the year is £7,500,000, with net assets of £1,650,000. Materiality has been set at £40,000.  



Outstanding matters from substantive testing 



Inventory 



There is a balance of £60,000 recorded in inventory relating to premium grain harvested in Autumn 20X3. This premium grain only achieves the top price if it is sold within six months of being harvested. It was noted during the course of the audit that Holly was trying to sell this grain for animal feed. Holly was negotiating a price of £30,000. 



Valuation of property 



A valuation report is due imminently for a section of farm buildings which Holly is considering selling to the Forestry Commission. A verbal report has indicated that the value may be £100,000 less than was paid by Holly upon acquisition.



Outstanding insurance claim 



During the course of the year, a tanker which was carrying the young trout to the fish farm ran off the road and crashed. The accident caused the trout to become agitated, and they all died. An insurance claim was submitted and negotiations regarding a pay-out were nearly completed at the time of the audit. The inventory was written off, but no income has been recognised in relation to the insurance claim. The fish had been purchased for £50,000. Yesterday Holly's finance director informed the audit partner that the claim has been settled in Holly's favour, and they expect the compensation to be deposited into their bank account within the next week.



Solution



Question 1:



Select the headings to review the audit procedures for each.





Selling grain as animal feed

Enquire of management whether the grain has been sold as animal feed since the year-end or whether it is likely to be sold and for what value.



If the grain has been sold, inspect the sales invoice and bank statement if paid, to support the net realisable value of the grain and compare to the carrying value to determine whether a write-down is required.



If the grain has not been sold, compare the value of the grain to the values for comparable grade grain per the commodity market or seek a professional valuation.





Valuer’s report

Obtain a written copy of the valuation report and inspect for caveats and other risks identified by the valuation professional.



Compare the carrying value of the property with the valuation to determine whether any impairment has occurred.





Outstanding insurance claim

Inspect court documents and documentation from Holly Farm’s lawyers and the insurance company relating to the insurance claim to confirm the value of the settlement agreed. 



Inspect bank statements for June 20X4 to agree receipt of the claim in the bank statement post-year-end.

Note 1 



The sale of inventory after the balance sheet date for an amount less than cost would normally be an event which requires an adjustment, as it demonstrates that the estimate of net realisable value at the period end was incorrect. 



This is not material, and no adjustment is required. 



(Note: as this item is not clearly trivial, the auditor would communicate this to Holly's management. However, if they choose not to adjust, this will have no impact on the audit opinion as the item is not material, or material in aggregate with other uncorrected differences. This will be covered in more detail in Module 10).



&#x20;



Note 2 



The valuation is lower than the carrying amount in the financial statements, so the property value should be impaired.  



As this misstatement is individually greater than overall materiality, it should be adjusted. 



Note 3 



The receipt of an insurance claim which was in the process of being finalised at the year-end would normally be an item which would require adjustment as the loss is already reflected in the income statement. 



This item should be adjusted by the audited entity as the adjustment is individually material.
