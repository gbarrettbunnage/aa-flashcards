#### Question 1



Read the following scenario.



You are an audit manager at Welsh and Harding LLP (W\&H), a firm of Chartered Accountants and registered auditors. You are currently working on the external audit of Byrds Bakes and Cakes Ltd (Byrds) for the year ending 31 August 20X8. Byrds is an established company and is a new audit client for your firm.



Byrds is a UK-based commercial bakery producing cakes and biscuits which are sold to supermarkets and independent grocers.



Steven Reay, the finance director of Byrds, has enquired about whether W\&H would be interested in carrying out an additional assignment. Byrds is introducing a new range called “Free as a Byrd” which will be made without gluten in order to cater for customers who have food intolerances. To finance the capital expenditure for the new range, Byrds is seeking a loan from its bank. Negotiations are progressing and recently the board received a request from its bank that W\&H, as auditors, provides assurance on the sales and cashflow forecasts Byrds has prepared to support its loan application. Steven has forwarded the bank’s letter (Appendix), which has been annotated with his comments. Your audit partner, Charlie Herron, has asked you to draft paragraphs for inclusion in a memo explaining the types of engagement that could be undertaken by W\&H, recommending the most appropriate for Byrds.



Question: 



Explain, with example opinions where appropriate, the different types of assurance engagement that could be undertaken in respect of the sales and cashflow forecasts to be provided to the bank, recommending the most appropriate type of engagement.



Appendix: Extracts of a letter from ScotWest Bank to Steven Reay and his comments thereon







We have reviewed the sales and cashflow forecasts for Byrds for the year to 31 August 20X9 you provided to support your request for the £500,000 loan. Some elements of these forecasts are based on the historical performance of Byrds, but the view of the bank is that the addition of the Free as a Byrd range represents a new business venture and we wish to seek further assurances on these forecasts before finalising the loan agreement.  



We are, therefore, proposing that you engage your external auditors, W\&H, and request that they certify the accuracy of the sales and cashflow forecasts for the year to 31 August 20X9.



We understand that you will need to give permission to W\&H for them to access and release confidential information to us here at ScotWest.







Steven’s comments

The new Free as a Byrd range will be heavily promoted to our existing customer base throughout October 20X8 and the sales forecasts assume that sales of the new range will commence on 1 November under our usual 60 days’ credit terms.



The sales and cashflow forecasts covering our existing business have been prepared using assumptions based on historical information, taking into account current year changes in customer and supplier payment terms. The cashflow forecasts include forecast capital expenditure, interest payments on the loan and all tax payments.



We have identified the machinery needed for production and plan to take delivery by the end of September. Production is planned to start in October to ensure products can be in shops before the crucial Christmas trading period. Solution

The following section presents an example of how you should structure your answer. 





Forms of engagement that may be considered



&#x20;



Reasonable assurance  



This would be an engagement where we provide reasonable assurance and a positive opinion. For example, “in our opinion, the sales and cashflow forecasts for Byrds for the year to 31 August 20X9 are accurate in all material respects, based on the criteria”. This form of report requires us to perform detailed testing to be able to reach a conclusion and, as a result, is more costly than the alternative engagement, limited assurance.   



&#x20;



Limited assurance  



This type of engagement provides limited assurance. For example, “based on our work described in this report, nothing has come to our attention that causes us to believe that the sales and cashflow forecasts for Byrds are not accurate in all material aspects, based on the criteria”. Because of the nature of the engagement, the testing procedures are less extensive and less reliable than under reasonable assurance.  



&#x20;



Recommendation  



We would be able to provide a limited assurance engagement as we could consider whether the underlying assumptions used to prepare the sales and cashflow forecasts are reasonable, consistent and consistently applied, and whether the assumptions used have been adequately disclosed.  



&#x20;



Forecasts are risky as they are based on the financial information prepared, with assumptions regarding future events that management expect to happen. Here, we are being asked to give assurance over forecast sales and cash flows, including a new range of products expected to start production in October. As such, there are too many uncertainties to reduce risk to an acceptable level to provide reasonable assurance.

APPENDIX: Extracts of notes from meeting with Lindsey Thomson



General information



Lindsey spends most of her time at the branch in Kensington. She employs a book-keeper, Nicola Skinner, who works at the Kensington office for 5 hours a day, Monday to Saturday. Timeout’s accounts are kept on a basic off-the-shelf accounting software package.



Revenue system in operation at the airport outlet



The new outlet opened on 1 August 20X2 and is open 7 days a week from 08:00 to 20:00. There is normally only one massage therapist working at any one time. The cash register allows the therapist to enter the value of the sale and the method of payment (cash or card). The outlet operates a chip and pin terminal for debit/credit card payments.



At the end of each shift, the therapist runs the till receipts total report, bags the cash and debit/credit card copy receipts with the till receipts total report and stores these in security bags with tamper-evident locks.



Once every 2 weeks, Lindsey visits the outlet to collect the daily takings. She takes these back to Kensington, placing them in the safe for Nicola to bank the cash. At the end of the month, Nicola opens the cash bags and reconciles each day’s till receipts total to the cash and debit/credit card payment slips. She enters the amount per the till receipts total report for each day into the nominal ledger for the outlet. The till receipts reconciliations are filed with the till receipts totals by date and by outlet.



The payment processing provider that Timeout uses for their debit/credit card transactions credits its bank account on a monthly basis and sends a remittance advice which provides the individual transactions comprising the total credited, ie each customer sale. Nicola files these reports on receipt.







Nicola closes the ledgers for the month and prepares a basic profit and loss account by the twentieth of the following month, therefore ensuring that the airport outlet’s takings are recorded in the correct month. Lindsey emails the revenue figure to the airport operator and also calculates the expected rent due. She uses this to validate the rent invoice she receives from the airport operator before authorising Nicola to process the rent payment. On a quarterly basis, Nicola performs a bank reconciliation which Lindsey reviews.







Rental agreement



Rent for the new outlet is based on revenue per the nominal ledger of Timeout’s airport outlet for the previous month. Rent is payable on the fifteenth of the next month.







For example, rent for April 20X3 was based on revenue for March 20X3, which was emailed to the airport operator in April and was payable on 15 May 20X3. Per the rental agreement, there is a fixed amount of rent payable each month of £500.







In addition to this, a variable amount of rent is also due at the following rates:



Total revenue less than £2,000: additional rent of 5% of outlet turnover 

Total revenue £2,000 – £3,000: additional rent of 6% of outlet turnover 

Total revenue greater than £3,000: additional rent of 7% of outlet turnover 

The rental agreement states that “the rent calculation and outlet turnover figure are to be certified separately by Timeout’s auditors”.



Solution

The following section presents an example of how you should structure your answer.



Types of engagement 

Before commencing the assignment, we will have to agree with Lindsey what type of assurance she will require. There are three options available to us.



Reasonable assurance 

This would be where we collect sufficient evidence to be able to provide an opinion stating that Timeout’s procedure for capturing and recording all revenue at the outlet accurately is functioning correctly. This form of report will require a high level of testing to be able to reach that conclusion and as a result will be more costly.



Limited assurance 

This is more limited. We would conclude that nothing has come to our attention to cause us to believe that the procedures for capturing and recording all revenue at the outlet accurately are not functioning correctly. Because of the nature of the engagement, the testing procedures would be less extensive and less reliable than those under positive assurance. This may not provide Lindsey with the level of assurance she requires for the airport operator.



Agreed-upon procedures 

A further option would be that our work would follow agreed-upon procedures. In our engagement letter, we would specify what procedures we were going to undertake. Our report would report those procedures and then outline our findings from each procedure. It is up to the user, the airport operator, to read the report and form their own conclusions on compliance based on the procedures undertaken and the findings.



Recommended report

I recommend we propose to Lindsey that we perform agreed-upon procedures. Auditors do not normally certify specific amounts payable to a third party, therefore, for clarity, it would be most appropriate for us to issue a short report to the airport operator detailing the procedures we performed on revenue and the results of those procedures.



Design procedures the firm could undertake 



&#x20;The types of procedures we undertake will depend on the final scope of the engagement. However, examples of procedures we could perform include:



Review of the rental agreement to verify the variable and fixed rentals due.

Recalculate a sample of rental invoices to ensure that the rental figure has been calculated accurately.

Agree a sample of outlet turnover figures used in the rental calculations to the totals per the sales ledger.

Trace back a sample of daily sales totals per the sales ledger to the till receipts totals report for that day.

Conduct a site visit to the outlet and verify the end-of-day activities being carried out. Perform a reconciliation between the till receipts totals and the physical cash and debit/credit card copy payment slips.

Review Nicola’s reconciliations of till receipts totals to the cash counted and the debit/credit card payment slips for that day and follow up any unreconciled differences.
