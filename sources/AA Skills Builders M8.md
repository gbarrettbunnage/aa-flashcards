Question 1 – Paragraphs for inclusion in the audit strategy memorandum regarding audit risks over PPE



Dundee building revaluation

The revaluation of the Dundee building was undertaken by Alfie’s friend. There is a risk that he is not independent of the board of Beeline and may not have provided an accurate valuation and, as a result, the building value could be over or understated.



Appropriateness of depreciation policy

PCV equipment has been sold for a significant gain of £70,000. This could indicate that the depreciation rates being used are inappropriate resulting in the carrying value of assets being misstated.



Impairment of PPE

The company does not appear to carry out any review of impairment of its PPE, including its PCVs which may reduce in value as a result of high usage. There is a risk that the carrying value of assets in the financial statements is overstated.



Accounting for leases on PCVs

Beeline has entered into lease contracts for its new PCVs, for the first time. These leases have been capitalised but there is a risk that these may not have been accounted for correctly as there are currently no additions recorded in the financial statements.



Diesel PCVs due to be scrapped

Four PCVs due to be scrapped have a net book value per the FAR of £167,000. It is unlikely that the scrappage value of these assets will be in line with the net book value indicating evidence of impairment and overstatement in the nominal ledger.

Question 2 – Sections for substantive audit testing programme to address audit risks identified



In the following section, we will recap how to design follow-up procedures in response to an identified audit risk. As a risk is identified the auditor would be looking to carry out a relevant test of detail. In some situations, common audit procedures are considered when designing an audit work programme.  However, often the procedure will need to be suitably tailored to reflect the information in the scenario.



In question 1 we evaluated the following risk:



Beeline has entered into lease contracts for its new PCVs, for the first time. These leases have been capitalised but there is a risk that these may not have been accounted for correctly, eg the cost capitalised may include interest.



A common test for PPE is to agree that all leased assets are accounted for correctly in the financial statements in accordance with accounting standards. However, it is important that we tailor this appropriately to the risk in the scenario. In this case, we need to check we are specifically testing the new leases for its PCVs. Tailoring the test is important so that it is clear what procedure is being performed and a well-designed, tailored audit procedure is normally worth two marks.



Have a look at the following example:



Confirm that the new leases for the hydrogen PCVs are accounted for in accordance with relevant IAS/IFRS.

#### Question 2



Read the following scenario.



You are the audit manager in a firm that has just acquired its first client with charitable status. Highland Healthcare (‘Highland’) provides both unskilled and professional volunteers to support the needs of the physically disabled living in the community.



At Highland’s head office, the accounting, commercial and publicity activities take place as follows:



All accounting activities are based on a manual cashbook with no payables or receivables ledgers;



Commercial activities comprise a tearoom and a charity shop selling second-hand clothes and second-hand furniture. Separate tills are held in the shop and the tearoom; and



A monthly newsletter is produced which publicises the charity’s activities and events around the region.



Highland receives local government funding, but this does not cover all of its financial obligations and it needs to organise regular fund-raising events and appeals throughout the year. It has also received occasional bequests from the estates of individuals. One particularly large bequest was received in the current year and this has been invested in the stock market on the advice of a local stockbroker who has agreed to provide a portfolio review service for the trustees.



There are six other offices throughout the country. They are run by unpaid voluntary staff and are used as bases for carers and as storage areas for donations of clothes and furniture for assessment locally before sending them on to the shop. These offices are also used by local committees that meet there to plan and organise fund-raising events.



You have concluded that you are happy with the quality of audit work and financial reporting in prior years from reviews of the outgoing auditor’s audit files, but that a major risk area for audit purposes is completeness of income. Your audit partner has asked you to design specific substantive procedures to cover the completeness of Highland’s various income streams.  They have advised that your audit junior has been tasked with covering some tests around the completeness of income and these have already been documented in the audit work programme. These are substantive analytical review procedures and tests of detail around any deferred income.



&#x20;



Question:



Design a list of substantive tests of detail to check for completeness of Highland’s income.



&#x20;



You should start with identifying Highland’s various income streams and then design tests of detail for each of these individually. Remember that it is only the completeness assertion that you are designing tests of detail for.



Solution



Please note that completeness of income would not normally be considered to be a critical risk issue for any investment-orientated commercial business seeking to expand its operations. However Highland Healthcare is a charity and on the basis of their understanding of the entity and risk assessment work, the auditors have deemed completeness of income to be a risk.



When designing substantive procedures we have only been asked to design procedures that would test the completeness assertion.  You should remember from the subject Assurance that income is a transaction and therefore its definition is as follows:



Completeness - Transactions, events and related disclosure that should have been recorded have been recorded.



Therefore, when designing tests around income the auditors are trying to obtain assurance that no income has been omitted from the financial statements that should have been recorded or disclosed.



When considering this scenario you need to identify the different streams of income that the entity receives.



This is to ensure that you design substantive procedures that cover all income streams. Let’s review the information from the scenario to identify Highland’s various income streams.

Example procedures that could be performed include:







Tearoom

For a sample of days throughout the year, inspect till receipts and confirm that cash received per the till receipts has been recorded and that the amount recognised is accurate.





Charity shop

Inspect details of donations received at the local offices and verify that these have an associated sale or are included in closing inventory to verify sales are completely recorded. Enquire with management regarding any irregularities.





Local government funding

Events and appeals

Bequests

Inspect correspondence from lawyers noting bequeathed amounts, checking all have been included in Highland’s income





Investment income

Obtain a listing of Highland’s portfolio and verify all dividends from each investment are correctly and fully included, by reference to external investment source (eg Financial Times)

Obtain a confirmation from the stockbroker indicating all investments held during the year and agree through review of stockbroker reports that investment income has been received and recorded completely. Discuss any omissions with the client to identify whether income was earned and should have been recognised
