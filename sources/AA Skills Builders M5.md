# AA Skills Builders M5



A scenario may indicate that particular laws and regulations are critical to the organisation. These may include health and safety, terms of a licence, financial services and environmental regulation. Where it is clear that a company is highly regulated and if auditors become aware of any actual or suspected non-compliance, you should refer to implications on audit work.



As with other audit risks, the relevant fact should be explained in terms of its impact on the financial statements, eg going concern or unrecognised liabilities/judgemental provisions and, if required, appropriate procedures should be designed in response to the risk of material misstatement.



Within an audit risk and approach question, the scenario provided may include, for example, the use of an accounting estimate, the use of an outsourced service provider, complex related party relationships or management’s use of an expert. You should recognise from the scenario that there is a specific auditing standard in relation to each of these matters and refer to the requirements of the standard in preparing your answer.



You may also be asked to evaluate the strengths and weaknesses of an audit client’s internal audit function and conclude on whether external auditors can rely on the work of internal audit.



For questions related to relying on the work of others (experts and internal auditors in particular), you’ll often be asked to conclude on whether reliance can be placed on their work. It’s important to give that conclusion as it is awarded separate marks in the AA assessment.



Question 1



Read the following scenario.



Dodling Drinks Ltd (Dodling) manufactures and exports a number of soft drinks. You are the audit partner responsible for the audit of the financial statements of Dodling for the year ending 31 December 20X4. It is October 20X4. Today, you attended a planning meeting with John Levin, the finance director at Dodling, and noted the following matters.



Internal audit



Since 1 January 20X4, Dodling has benefited from a newly formed internal audit function. John asked if the audit team can use the work of this function to increase the efficiency of the external audit.  



John was responsible for appointing all members of the internal audit function, but the team now reports to the audit committee (formed in June 20X4 to prepare Dodling for listing). The internal audit function comprises the head of internal audit, an internal audit manager and a trainee internal auditor.  



Bobby Gross, the head of internal audit, joined Dodling after 10 years as an internal auditor at a food manufacturer. John believes Dodling are lucky to have such an experienced individual at the company. Bobby is a member of the Institute of Internal Auditors. 



Grace O'Brien is the internal audit manager. Grace currently has a dual role, as she is splitting her time between internal audit and acting as finance manager for Dodling. The permanent finance manager is away on maternity leave until November 20X4 and it was decided that Grace was best placed to temporarily cover the role for the 3 months from September to November 20X4. Grace had no prior internal audit experience before joining Dodling but is a qualified Chartered Accountant. Grace has requested we let her know which areas of internal audit work we would like in advance. She has struggled to review and file all work papers while carrying out both roles at the same time and would like time to prepare for our visit.  



&#x20;



Property revaluation 



John has forwarded the following email with details of a revaluation at Dodling. 



To:       Grace O'Brien 



From:  John Levin 



Re:       Property valuation 



&#x20;



Grace, 



We have used my cousin's property company, Gary Property Ltd (GP) to perform a valuation of the Aberdeen factory premises for the purpose of financial statement preparation. I think you've met my cousin Gary already as he has a 5% stake in Dodling. GP has 20 years' experience in the commercial property market and has a great reputation. I've attached their report here. Please make the appropriate adjustments to increase the value of the factory in our books.



Regards, 



John



Payroll services 



In the year, Dodling’s main payroll manager retired and, as a result, a decision was made to outsource the payroll process to an external organisation, We Help You Pay Ltd (WHYP). 



John has asked you to provide an overview of the work required to determine if reliance can be placed on WHYP for the purpose of the audit as this is the first time they have engaged an external organisation to provide such a service. 



&#x20;



Legal case 



John explained that in the last month, a customer has sought legal action against Dodling as they discovered a foreign object in one of their soft drinks. The case is in the early stages, and John is still unclear how this could have occurred but assumes there was an error in the manufacturing process and that the legally required process was not followed. It is unlikely the case will be settled before the year end. John explained that Dodling's finance team have calculated an initial estimate of a provision which may be required in the financial statements.

Solution

Question 1

Internal audit 







Objectivity

The internal audit team were appointed by John Levin, finance director at Dodling.

The appointment by the finance director raises issues of objectivity as John Levin may be able to influence the work of the internal audit function, eg if he has an ongoing relationship with any team members. The appointment should have been made by the audit committee and there should be established policies for hiring internal auditors.

Grace O'Brien is temporarily carrying out a dual function as finance manager at Dodling and also internal audit manager.

It is likely that Grace will be reviewing work which she has completed herself, or that of her colleagues, while she is temporarily finance manager, for example, where she has been asked to process the revaluation of the factory. This may mean that she is less likely to spot errors and/or be willing to raise concerns as her objectivity will likely be impaired.

The internal audit function reports to the audit committee at Dodling. 



Note: In evaluating whether reliance can be placed, you are awarded marks for drawing out the strengths as well.

The fact that the internal audit function now reports to those charged with governance at Dodling will positively influence the external auditors in their decision on whether to rely on the internal audit function at Dodling as this protects the objectivity of the function.

Competence

Grace O'Brien has no prior experience as an internal auditor but is a qualified CA.

While Grace is qualified and likely to have the relevant technical competence, she does not have specific expertise in internal audit, which may reduce her ability to effectively contribute to the IAF.

Bobby Gross is an experienced internal auditor and a member of the Institute of Internal Auditors.

An experienced head of internal audit will increase the external auditor’s comfort in relying on the work of internal audit as this is an indication of his competence.

The internal audit department has three members of staff.

The size of the internal audit department is likely to be insufficient for the size of the organisation (since the company is planning to list) which may impact their ability to sufficiently cover all the required areas necessary for external audit reliance.

Systematic and disciplined approach

Grace O'Brien has not had time to maintain the internal audit files.

Not filing work papers in a timely fashion shows poor quality management and increases the chance of the internal audit function's work papers being incomplete or lost. It will be difficult for the auditors to place reliance on internal audit if there is a lack of sufficient appropriate audit evidence to support any conclusions made.





Conclusion







The issues relating to having a systematic and disciplined approach seem to be temporary and only applicable for the 3 months Grace O'Brien has been carrying out a dual role. Therefore, work carried out by the internal audit function for the remaining 9 months of the year may be more reliable and could in principle be used by the external auditors. 







Although the internal audit team is technically competent, their objectivity is highly likely to be impaired. It is unlikely that this threat can be overcome without a change in the members of the internal audit team. 







Concerns over quality management and the objectivity of the internal function may make it unlikely that we can place reliance on the work of the internal audit function.





Question 2

Revaluation 







The auditors need to consider whether they can place reliance on the work of GP, a management expert.







Objectivity 







The revaluation of the property has been carried out by GP, which is not an independent management expert as Gary, the owner, owns a 5% share of Dodling. Gary therefore stands to profit from the listing of Dodling and would personally benefit from Dodling having a higher valuation. 



&#x20;



Note: Gary is not a related party as he is not classed as ‘close family’ and does not have control (only 5% shares of Dodling).







Competence and capabilities 







Based on John’s summary, the competence appears to be sufficient, with GP having a strong reputation and experience in the area of commercial property valuation. However, this may be a biased assessment and we will need to perform additional procedures to understand the competence and capability of GP, including reviewing professional qualifications or memberships, the use of standards by GP and experience in valuing properties for the purpose of  IFRS® Accounting Standards  accounting adjustments.







Further considerations and approach 



The motivation for management for the revaluation may be to improve the view presented in the financial statements in light of the listing – there may be an element of management bias in the decision to revalue. The risks associated with management bias mean that the auditors will need to carefully consider the source of evidence they obtain when looking at the valuation of the factory – a preference for third-party or auditor-generated evidence will be key. The auditors must maintain an attitude of professional scepticism when carrying out audit procedures on the revaluation amount and raise the risk of material misstatement due to increased fraud risk factors elsewhere in the audit. Auditors need to ensure that all assets in that category (ie properties) have been subjected to revaluation. 



&#x20;



Conclusion





It is unlikely that the auditors would place reliance on the work of GP due to the issues raised above and they would need to carry out their own audit procedures on the factory valuation; this may include using an auditor expert to assess the reasonableness of the property valuation.





Question 3

Use of a service organisation 







We must obtain a full understanding of the nature of the payroll services provided and the contractual terms around the service level agreement between Dodling and WHYP. 



&#x20;



Our approach depends on the level of interaction and design and operating effectiveness of controls put in place by Dodling over the submission and receipt of payroll information. We must also obtain an understanding of the materiality of the payroll expense.



Where we cannot obtain a sufficient understanding of the controls over the payroll information from Dodling alone, we should:



Obtain a report from WHYP which includes a description of their controls and a report by their auditor on their controls.

Contact WHYP to obtain specific information.

Visit WHYP.

Use another auditor to assess the design of the controls at WHYP.

As Dodling’s auditor, once we have assessed the risks around the use of a service organisation for preparing the company’s payroll, we should determine whether tests of controls and substantive testing of information created by WHYP, but held at Dodling, provides us with sufficient appropriate audit evidence. If not, we must perform further audit procedures.



&#x20;



If we decide to rely on controls at WHYP, we must obtain evidence that they operated effectively during the period. For example, by obtaining a report from the financial consultant’s auditor providing assurance over the operations of controls at the service organisation (ie a Type 2 report).

Question 2



You are the audit manager for the audit of Cellz Science Services Ltd (Cellz). 



Cellz is a life sciences company employing a large number of staff, including production personnel and trained scientists. Cellz produces a diagnostic blood testing machine that is used at home to monitor sugar levels in diabetic patients. As well as production facilities, Cellz also has a growing IT engineering function as the company is developing electronic monitoring equipment.



Cellz is an existing audit client and the audit partner has asked you to evaluate its internal audit function (IAF) to determine whether or not your firm can place reliance on its work for the purpose of the external audit. Your senior has prepared some notes for you:  



&#x20;



IAF members  



&#x20;



The IAF comprises of four members. The head of the IAF has recently joined Cellz from a well-known large audit firm and has a number of years of experience in internal audit.



The other three members have been working in Cellz’s IAF for a number of years:  



One team member is a qualified accountant, who also has a qualification in IT audit 



The remaining two members are currently undertaking professional training as internal auditors and were recruited externally, with one being the son of the finance director 



The head of IAF reports directly to the chairman of the audit committee.  



IAF work programme 



&#x20;



Over the past 2 years, the IAF has performed nearly 20 reviews.  



The previous head of the IAF decided, in discussion with the directors throughout the year, the nature and timing of all reviews carried out.  



The IAF has undertaken work and issued reports in the last 2 years over key business processes, such as payroll, purchasing and financial reporting. In addition, and in response to specific requests from the directors, the IAF has undertaken one-off reviews which have included a fraud investigation and a post-implementation system review.  



Review of the reports and working papers showed that the reviews have been completed following well-constructed audit programmes which refer to internal audit standards.  



During my review, I did not note evidence of any external quality management processes in place. 



Question: 



Evaluate the strengths and weaknesses of the internal audit function of Cellz and conclude on whether reliance can be placed on the work of the internal audit function for the purpose of the external audit.



Solution



In order to comply with ISA (UK) 610 Using the Work of Internal Auditors, we need to consider the adequacy of the internal audit function before placing reliance on its work as part of the audit engagement. From the audit team’s discussion with internal audit team members, and from review of working files and reports, we have noted the following: 





Strengths of Cellz’s IAF

The head of internal audit is an experienced internal audit professional with training and experience.



The team appears to be sufficiently trained, with the team members either holding professional qualifications (two members) or relevant experience of the company (two members).



There is specific IT audit experience in the team, which is important with the growing IT engineering function of Cellz.



The IAF has independent reporting lines and accountability which support the objectivity in the IAF’s approach and conclusions as its head reports directly to the chairman of the audit committee.



The IAF has carried out a wide range of reviews over the last 2 years, including coverage of key business processes as well as individual reviews.



Audit work programmes appear to be formal and structured, with appropriate reference to internal audit standards.





Weaknesses of IAF

There is a potential conflict of interest with a team member who is related to the finance director.  This would need to be considered in more detail, including a review of working files that the team member prepared and evidence of additional review.  



There is a risk of lack of independence of the IAF’s work, as the focus of the IAF work has been guided by the directors to whom it reports, rather than following an audit plan.   



A risk-based approach to audit planning has not been adopted and no audit programme for the year has been prepared and agreed with the audit committee. There is a risk that the highest risk business processes have not been subject to internal audit review and that the coverage of key financial statement areas may not be sufficient to place reliance.  



Effective quality management processes do not appear to be in place. There may be no external scrutiny of the work of the team.





Conclusion

Although there are a number of strengths to the IAF, we cannot place reliance on its work at the present time as historically the work carried out was following discussions with directors rather than following an audit plan that adopted a risk-based approach.



&#x20; 



OR







Based on the strengths above, reliance may be able to be placed on the work of internal audit subject to the following additional areas of confirmation:



Review of coverage of internal audit against overall audit strategy and audit plan.



Specific review of audit working papers, report and conclusions for any areas where reliance may be placed. If any concerns are raised, perform additional audit procedures in these areas.



Ensure adequate mitigating oversight controls are in place over the work of the related party employed within the IAF to protect the objectivity and independence of the IAF function.
