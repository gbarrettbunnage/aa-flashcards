#### Question 1



Read the following scenario. 



&#x20;



You are an audit senior at Hill and Jones LLP (HJ) working on the year-end audit of the financial statements of WindCable Engineering Limited (Wind) for the year ended 31 December 20X0. You are preparing to audit depreciation charges.



Wind is a UK-based engineering company which specialises in the engineering, project management and installation of subsea cables that are required to transport onshore the energy generated by the offshore wind turbines. Due to the nature of the work, Wind has tangible assets which are mobilised onto offshore vessels to perform the required work. Wind does not own or operate any of the vessels; these are contracted separately from the vessel owner by Wind’s customer.  



The audit planning and interim work are complete, and you have concluded that the controls over the depreciation charges are robust. As a result, the planned audit approach is to perform a substantive analytical review (SAR), with testing at an individual asset level. Tolerable error has been set at £7,500. 



Your audit manager, Janet Bennett, has provided you with an extract from Wind’s asset register (Appendix 1), including details of Wind’s tangible asset accounting policies, which she received following a meeting with Wind’s senior accountant, Benjamin Cormack. She has also provided you with a note of other relevant information obtained during that meeting (Appendix 2).  



Janet has asked you to perform the SAR to evaluate the reasonableness of the charges based on the information gathered. She would also like you, based on this information, to analyse the potential reasons for any variances between your expectations and the actual depreciation charges for the year.  



&#x20;



Questions: 



Using the information provided: 



Prepare information, suitable for inclusion in an audit workpaper, in which you perform a substantive analytical review to evaluate the reasonableness of the depreciation charges.



Analyse the potential reasons for any variances between the expected and actual depreciation charges for the year.



Tutor tip:



In this question, we have been asked to perform a substantive analytical review of the depreciation charged in relation to a company’s tangible assets. In the coming appendices, we will see that there are four large assets, each with their own charge.   



There are two requirements. The first involves evaluating the reasonableness of the company’s depreciation charges.   



The recommended answering technique for doing this is as follows:  



Step 1: Read the scenario carefully and form an expectation. This will involve calculating an expected depreciation charge for each asset, using the information provided. This includes information such as the cost of assets, when they came into use and the company’s depreciation policies.   



Step 2: Compare the expectation to the actual results given in the scenario. We should compare our own expectations, developed at step 1, with the actual results – the depreciation charges applied by the company. These will often differ; in which case, we should calculate the size of the variance. 



Step 3: Conclude on which balances require further investigation. Where there is a variance that is lower than the ‘tolerable error’ threshold (£7,500 in this case, as stated in the question) there is no need to investigate further as we have obtained the required level of assurance about the reasonableness of the depreciation charge. For variances greater than the threshold, we should clearly state a conclusion that further investigation is required. 



The second requirement involves analysis of the potential reasons for any variances between the expected and actual depreciation charges for the year.



To meet this requirement, we should continue to look separately at each asset for which the variance exceeded the threshold. We should use the information in the scenario, combined with our own understanding of how depreciation should be calculated (and thus potential risks to an accurate calculation) to consider potential reasons for the variance.



When you are reviewing the solution provided for this question, please note how the structure of the solution follows the steps set out above. When you are reviewing your own answer, your calculations may differ from those in the solution. As long as you applied the process above, you would still be able to gain some credit in an assessment if you provided appropriate evaluation of your own calculations, even if those calculations included some errors.

A purchase order for the super dredger was raised in August 20X0. The subsea plough is the first leased equipment taken on by Wind. All other equipment has been purchased outright by Wind. The asset value was recognised based on the lease liability at the commencement of the lease, adjusted for lease payments made before commencement of the lease and direct costs incurred by Wind. The term of the lease as per the agreement is 5 years.



The cable carousel was manufactured by Wind and includes £780,000 of external contractor costs, including components and materials, £90,000 of marketing costs, £50,000 of internal labour costs and £10,000 of delivery costs. It was transferred from construction in progress (CIP) on 1 January 20X0.



The warehouse and related land are the first purchased property for Wind. All properties have previously been rented on short-term leases. Based on the property surveyor’s report, the land had a value of £365,000 at the date of purchase.



Wind’s tangible asset accounting policies



All assets are to be depreciated using the straight-line method, except buildings, which are to be depreciated at 5% per annum using the diminishing balance method. Land is not depreciated.



Depreciation is to be charged from the first full month of ownership. No depreciation is charged in the first month if the date purchased or date transferred from CIP is not the first day of the month.



Depreciation is to be charged in full in the month of disposal regardless of the date of disposal. 



The start date for depreciation of an asset is determined by the date that the assets are available for use by Wind. This is indicated by the date of the signed goods received note from the Wind stores department for purchased goods, the start date of the lease agreement for leased assets or the date that goods are transferred from CIP for those manufactured by Wind. 



The end date for depreciation of an asset is determined by the date of the signed goods despatch note from the Wind stores department.



Costs that can be capitalised in assets manufactured by Wind can include external contractor costs, delivery costs, allocated property taxes during the period of construction, installation costs and internal labour. Excluded costs include those relating to marketing and asset repairs.

Analysis of variances



Select each heading.





Super dredger

The asset was purchased in October 20X0 while Benjamin was on sick leave and his duties were performed by the finance assistant. It is likely that the incorrect depreciation period has been used as the asset was purchased mid-month and 3 months’ depreciation may have been charged rather than 2.  



Subsea plough

The December returned payment for the asset of £19,500 was incorrectly recorded as a credit against the income statement depreciation charge for the year. This change may still not have been processed. This is also the first time that Wind has entered into a lease arrangement and this may result in the calculation of the asset cost being incorrect, leading to incorrect deprecation charges.



Cable carousel

The depreciation may be overstated as a result of marketing costs of £90,000 being included in the cost of the asset, which should have been excluded from the asset cost in line with the company’s accounting policy.  



Warehouse and land

The depreciation is overstated and would appear to be a result of land being included within the cost, which is not in line with the company’s accounting policy or accounting standards.

You are an audit manager at A\&A Chartered Accountants. You are currently undertaking the audit of Whistle Steel Manufacturers plc (WSM) for the year ended 31 October 20X2. &#x20;

Administration staff – employees in sales and administration teams 



Production staff – employees within the company’s manufacturing plant, including contractors 



Executive staff – executive directors’ remuneration

&#x20;



Your audit partner, Trisha Reynolds, has asked you to perform a substantive analytical review (SAR) to evaluate the reasonableness of the cost components for each payroll category relating to administration and production staff. Executive directors’ remuneration is being considered separately by the audit team and does not need to be covered as part of your work. She would like you to analyse the reasons for any difference between your expectations and the actual costs for the year ended 31 October 20X2. 



&#x20;



To assist with your work, you have extracted information from the prior year audit file in relation to payroll together with background information provided by WSM’s payroll manager regarding payroll costs during 20X1/X2 (Appendix 2). 



&#x20;

&#x20;



Questions:



Using the information provided:



Perform a substantive analytical review to evaluate the reasonableness of each payroll category relating to administration and production staff for the year ended 31 October 20X2.

Tutor tip:



In this question, we have been asked to perform a substantive analytical review of a company’s payroll expense, with appendices setting out further information on the company’s payroll and the basis for its calculation.  



As with the prior practice question (Wind) there are two requirements. The first involves evaluating the reasonableness of the company’s depreciation charges.



Remember that the recommended answering technique for doing this is as follows: 



Step 1: Read the scenario carefully and form an expectation.



Step 2: Compare the expectation to the actual results given in the scenario.



Step 3: Conclude on which balances require further investigation. In this case, the tolerable error has been set at £30,000. Again, for variances greater than the threshold, we should clearly state a conclusion that further investigation is required.



The second requirement involves analysis of the potential reasons for any variances between the expected and actual payroll expense. As with the previous practice question, we should use the information in the scenario, combined with our own accounting knowledge, to consider potential reasons for variances in excess of the tolerable error threshold.

Analysis of variances



Select each heading.





Private healthcare benefit (administration only)

The SAR expectation for administration payroll includes healthcare benefits introduced for all administration payroll staff in November 20X1.  



However, management has advised that an error was made during 20X2/X3, with additional costs being incurred by the company in respect of family members, which we would expect to result in actual costs being higher than our calculated expectation.



Statutory redundancy payments (administration only)

12 employees from the company’s marketing and promotion’s team received redundancy payments in April 20X2, which are not reflected in our calculated expectation.



Administration payroll employer’s national insurance and pension contributions (administration only)

Our expectations for these contributions are lower than the client figure. This may be caused, at least in part, by the absence of redundancy payments (which incur such contributions) from our calculated expectation.



Hourly pay rates (production only)

The use of average total pay for production staff does not take into account that hourly pay rates vary between staff depending on their grade level.



This may have the effect of increasing or decreasing our expectation of production pay depending on actual rates and any turnover of staff during the year.
