Question 2

In the following section, we will recap the scenario and notes. Fraud risk factors identified have been highlighted in the scenario and their explanation and evaluation are explained in comments.







Notes on revenues and cash







Donations and fundraising

Donations continue to be collected via monthly standing orders and ad hoc, by post and through the Park website. These amounts are paid directly to Park’s bank by donors’ banks, card issuers and various online payment services. Fundraising includes a range of high-profile sponsored events.







Park now employs an RGL who is personally responsible for driving both donation and fundraising income. A significant proportion of the RGL’s pay is performance-related and tied to the income generated, including gift aid generated. (Comments: this provides an incentive to fraudulently manipulate reported donations and fund income, which drives rewards to the RGL). 







Gift aid can be claimed from the government on eligible donations. Conditions include a declaration from donors that certain eligibility criteria have been met. The RGL has launched a drive to improve the rate of gift aid collection and the RGL receives and inputs all paper-based gift aid forms into Park’s finance system. (Comments: the lack of segregation of duties in the processing of gift aid creates a general risk of fraud. More specifically, the RGL would have an incentive and the opportunity to create fake gift aid forms or enter gift aid details that do not match the form to maximise their remuneration).







Legacies

These relate to sums left to Park in the wills of deceased supporters. The value of legacies in the first six months of the financial year is low but management is unconcerned as legacy cash flow is inherently ‘lumpy’ and no major income from this source had been anticipated. Procedures are largely unchanged from prior years. Money is normally paid directly from the deceased’s lawyers to Park’s bank at the end of the probate process.



Park’s trustees have a policy of not accepting any legacies or donations with conditions attached as they feel that this impairs Park’s independence and strategic flexibility.











Cash 

Cash handling from trading activities (shop and café) has fallen due to card and device payments, but is still significant, as are cash donations at all locations.



Cash collected at all sites is banked locally by site managers and a daily banking report is emailed to Park’s bookkeeper, who saves all such reports on Park’s cloud drive. (Comments: no one is monitoring these reports. No bank reconciliation is being prepared. This presents an opportunity for staff to fail to bank all monies and misappropriate the remainder without being detected). Prior to banking, cash at head office is stored in a safe. Only the bookkeeper and site manager are meant to have access, but there seems to be an ‘in joke’ around the office that the bookkeeper is an avid football fan and that the safe code was bound to be the year that their football team was founded. (Comments: this would allow unauthorised persons to access and misappropriate Park’s cash).







I did ask to see inside the safe and noticed that, as well as the cash and related paperwork collected by Park, there were two large envelopes containing money collected from staff for a lottery syndicate and the office Christmas lunch. Neither envelope contained an accounting record of the money inside. (Comments: monies that do not belong to Park are stored in envelopes in the safe, with no accounting records in place. This creates an opportunity to perpetrate a theft).







The bookkeeper seemed very irritable and mentioned to me that Park had previously employed a part-time financial controller but, due to a need for efficiencies, that post had been made redundant. The bookkeeper took on the controller’s responsibilities but has had no pay rise to reflect this. (Comments: this creates a potential rationalisation for misappropriation of assets, which the bookkeeper would be in a position to perpetrate given their role). 







One of their colleagues mentioned that the bookkeeper had been so busy that they had not taken a single day of leave since taking on these additional responsibilities. (Comments: this is a common indicator of fraud, as it provides an opportunity to cover up fraudulent activity by keeping other staff members away from activities that might result in the detection of a fraud).



The following section presents an example of how you should structure your answer.



Fraud risk factors





RGL

A significant proportion of the RGL’s pay is performance-related. This provides an incentive to fraudulently manipulate reported donations and fund income, which drive reward to the RGL. 





Gift aid

The RGL is incentivised to maximise gift aid collection and is personally responsible for receiving and manually inputting gift aid details from donors.







The lack of segregation of duties in the processing of gift aid creates a general risk of fraud. More specifically, the RGL would have an incentive and the opportunity to create fake gift aid forms or enter gift aid details that do not match the form to maximise their remuneration.





Cash

Significant cash amounts are still dealt with at all shop and café locations.  Cash banked locally is reported but these reports are not monitored with no reconciliation to bank statements. This presents an opportunity for staff to fail to bank all monies and misappropriate the remainder without being detected.





Safe code

The safe code has a code that is thought to be short and detectable. This would allow unauthorised persons to access and misappropriate Park’s cash.





​Other cash held in safe

Monies that do not belong to Park are stored in envelopes in the safe, with no accounting records in place. This creates an opportunity to perpetrate a theft. A staff member with access to the safe could place Park’s money in an envelope and, once investigations into the discrepancy had been investigated, remove the money from the envelope at a later date.





Bookkeeper

The bookkeeper is resentful that they have taken on extra responsibilities without extra pay. This creates a potential rationalisation for misappropriation of assets, which the bookkeeper would be in a position to perpetrate given their role. 







The bookkeeper has not taken any leave since taking on additional responsibilities. This is a common indicator of fraud, as it provides an opportunity to cover up fraudulent activity by keeping other staff members away from activities that might result in the detection of a fraud.

Read the following scenario. 



You are the audit senior on the audit of the financial statements of Petro-Chem Ltd (Petro-Chem) for the year ending 31 March 2X20. Your firm, Brennan and Neale (B\&N) has been the auditor of Petro-Chem for a number of years. This is your second year as audit senior and you are beginning the planning of this year’s audit. The principal activity of Petro-Chem is the processing and sale of chemicals to customers in the UK from a plant based in Liverpool. During the year, Petro-Chem has expanded and established a division in Bogotá, Colombia, which sells processed chemicals to customers in South America.



&#x20;



Your audit partner, Mike Brennan, recently held a planning meeting with the new chief financial officer (CFO) of Petro-Chem, Ian Mackenzie, where he explained developments in the business over the last six months. The expansion into South America has resulted in revenue increasing by 30% in the current year. However, Ian noted that a number of customers of the Bogotá division are very slow to pay and this is affecting Petro-Chem’s cash flow. 



&#x20;



Petro-Chem is already one of B\&N’s largest clients in terms of fee income when considering both audit and existing tax services. Following the meeting, Mike has asked you to evaluate the audit risks for Petro-Chem, including those arising from the changes in the company’s operations during the year. He has also requested that you recommend the audit approach for each audit risk. 



&#x20;



At the meeting, Ian also explained that, prior to shipping the processed chemicals to Colombia, significant quantities of chemicals are now stored by Petro-Chem in sealed drums in an underground facility at the Liverpool site. He explained that some of the chemicals can degrade over time and he is having difficulty in accurately estimating the value of these chemicals for inclusion in the inventory figure in the financial statements. Ian explained that he commissioned the services of a close friend to assess the value of the chemicals, who he insisted was appropriately qualified. 



&#x20;



Mike has raised concerns over the subjective method that has been used to value the chemicals and the apparent lack of objectivity of the expert used by Ian and has suggested that it might be appropriate to also seek expert advice. 



&#x20;



Business background



Petro-Chem was incorporated 14 years ago. The company processes and sells specialist chemicals to customers who operate in the oil and gas industry. The company has expanded recently into South America following significant interest from their largest customers, which have subsidiaries based there. 





&#x20;In response, the company has opened a division in Bogotá, Colombia, to allow for direct contact with these subsidiaries and other potential customers. Sales made from this division have accounted for a significant proportion of revenue in the current year.



Petro-Chem refines chemicals imported from a third party in Germany and sells them to customers in the UK and now also, through its division, to customers in South America. Industry practice in Colombia is that a volume discount is offered to all major customers. Ian is of the opinion that this will be calculated once all the relevant information has been received from Colombia and will be posted through next year’s accounts. 



&#x20;



Key operational issues



The following operational matters have arisen in the current year:



&#x20;



the construction of a blending tank to manufacture a specialised chemical for a one-off customer contract has been put on hold as the customer has gone into administration



&#x20;



month-end results for Colombia are recorded in peso (local currency) and are translated to sterling for reporting purposes 



&#x20;



Financial performance



Profit for the current year is behind budget with the following factors having an impact:



&#x20;



increased competition from alternative chemical processors in China, which is driving prices down 



sales volumes are lower than budget in Colombia



raw chemicals purchased from Germany are invoiced to Petro-Chem in euros. Due to currency fluctuations, prices paid have been higher than budgeted. Petro-Chem is considering a forward contract to buy euros and mitigate its exposure to foreign currency movements which have arisen



&#x20;



It was also noted that trade receivables days (184 days, prior year: 100 days) are significantly higher than trade payables days (63 days, prior year: 61 days) resulting in a deteriorating cash balance, which is forecast to be in overdraft by the year-end.
