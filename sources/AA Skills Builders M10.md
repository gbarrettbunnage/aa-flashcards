# AA Skills Builders M10



Introduction

AA assessment questions set at the completion and reporting stage of the audit often test various topics in combination.



It is important to ensure that you read the questions carefully to understand the assessor’s expectations.



For questions on going concern, if you are asked to evaluate indicators from the scenario that may lead to going concern problems for the client, ensure that you explain ‘why’ the indicator may cause liquidity or other related problems in the next twelve months. Procedures to evaluate the management’s going concern assessment need to be tailored to the scenario to ensure that your answer is relevant. If asked to evaluate the impact on the audit report, this needs to be carefully assessed. For example, if you agree with the management’s assessment, the opinion will not be modified in this regard but the relevant section of the audit report will be modified.



For questions on subsequent events, you may be asked to evaluate whether an event is adjusting or non-adjusting. Ensure that you explain ‘why’ as you are unlikely to be awarded full marks if you simply write that it’s one or the other. You may also be required to design further audit procedures to allow you to reach a conclusion on the required accounting treatment. The audit procedures will need to be tailored to the circumstances within the scenario in order to maintain the relevance of your answer to the question.



The impact on audit reports is often tested in combination with the evaluation of matters identified at the completion stage of the audit. These matters may include results of going concern or subsequent events reviews or those of evaluating the evidence related to misstatements or inability to gather sufficient appropriate evidence. For all questions, sound knowledge of the contents of unmodified and modified audit reports is the basic prerequisite.



#### Question 1



Read the following scenario.



You are the audit manager on the audit of the financial statements of Gelding Pharmaceuticals Plc (‘GP’) for the year ended 30 April 20X1. Following completion of the year-end audit fieldwork, you are reviewing the list of subsequent events prepared after a meeting between your audit partner Sally Breeder, James Foal, GP’s deputy finance director, and Edward Johnson, operations director.  



&#x20;



GP is a pharmaceutical research, development, production and testing company, whose customers comprise both development centres such as universities, and international distributors. The company operates from its headquarters in Sheffield but attracts worldwide clients. GP has research hubs in various locations throughout the world, which are focused on sourcing ingredients for future developments. The company is fully licensed and has been operating successfully for 12 years. The company was listed on the Alternative Investment Market on 1 May 20X1.  



The identified subsequent events have been documented by James and he has included commentary on whether he believes each event will require an adjustment to the financial statements. Sally has also reviewed the items and has added additional comments as necessary (Appendix). 



Sally has asked you to prepare paragraphs for inclusion in a briefing note, evaluating whether or not these are adjusting events. She also wants you to consider what additional audit evidence, if any, would be required for each of these matters. 



&#x20;



Questions: 



For each subsequent event detailed in the Appendix, you are required to: 



Evaluate the subsequent event, concluding whether or not it would be an adjusting event 



Consider what, if any, additional audit evidence would be required 



Evaluate the impact on the auditor's opinion and audit report to be issued 









APPENDIX

APPENDIX - List of subsequent events provided by James Foal, deputy finance director of GP, prepared as at 3 June 20X1 



&#x20;



Note: Overall materiality has been set at £135,000. GP’s profit before income taxes is £2.7 million and the company had net assets of £20 million as at 30 April 20X1. 



&#x20;



1\. Deterioration of inventory due to inappropriate storage



As a large batch of vaccine TG26 was being prepared for distribution in early May 20X1, it was noticed that the vaccines had gone cloudy within the vials. This would indicate that the vials had not been stored at a sufficiently low temperature, and further investigation into the maintenance logs indicated that there had been an issue with the power to the fridge in which the vaccine had been stored towards the end of March. Improper storage of this vaccine reduces its efficacy to only 10%. TG26 was the only vaccine stored in this fridge and the contents amounted to a value of £170,000.  



&#x20;

James’ comments



“This is not a normal event – we take great care in maintaining the environment in which we store our products. Investigation by our maintenance department indicated that this particular issue was caused by a blown fuse in the fridge in question which tripped the breaker on the fuse board. Maintenance remedied the matter with the fuse board as soon as they became aware of it. Due to the reduced efficacy of the vaccines these were destroyed on discovery in May 20X1.” 



Sally’s comments 



“None of these vaccines were distributed, so there will be no issues with complaints from GP’s customers.” 



&#x20;



2\. Administration of client



Handler Ltd, a distribution client of GP, went into Administration on 1 June 20X1. As at 30 April 20X1 the balance owed to GP was £115,000. 



&#x20;



James’ comments 



“The Board was aware that Handler had been experiencing financial difficulties, but did not deem this to be a significant issue until we received the notification from the administrator. Handler has made no additional orders since the year-end, but we did receive a payment of £45,000 on 12 May 20X1. The administrator has indicated that anticipated payments to creditors will be at a value of 2p in the £1.”



&#x20;



3\. Future production impacted by falsified research results 



Notice was received by GP in early May 20X1 that a project to produce a drug will no longer go ahead due to the fact that the testing company, Marrow Ltd, had falsified some of their research results on the efficacy of the drug. As a result, future planned production of this drug has been cancelled completely. 



&#x20;



James’ comments 



“This is a very disappointing development as we had invested £280,000 in specialised equipment to produce this drug. This equipment is not applicable to our current production requirements, although we are in discussions with another company, Consolidated Medications Ltd, on the possibility of producing a new product for them later this year. They are working on a project very similar to that of Marrow Ltd but are further behind in the process due to their rigorous research testing regime. Due to this proposed future use, no adjustments have been made to the financial statements for this matter. No products had yet been produced for Marrow Ltd.” 



&#x20;



4\. Listing of Gelding Pharmaceuticals on 1 May 20X1 



GP became listed on the AIM as of 1 May 20X1. This had been the intention of the business for several years. 



&#x20;



James’ comments 



“We were very happy with how the process of listing the company was actioned, and we are already undertaking appropriate training on the additional reporting requirements for the next financial year. We have, of course, made a brief disclosure that the company became listed in the notes to the financial statements.” 



&#x20;



5\. Destruction of one of the production facilities due to flooding 



Due to heavy rainfall experienced in early May 20X1, the River Don burst its banks, flooding one of GP’s production facilities in Inverurie, Scotland. Most of the equipment was destroyed.  



&#x20;



James’ comments 



“The site that was flooded was one of our smaller sites, although this does still amount to damage totalling £195,000. Fortunately, this will all be covered by our insurance policy. There was minimal inventory on site as we had just delivered the project that the site had been working on, and we have managed to accommodate current projects at our other production facilities. A disclosure note has been included in the financial statements in respect of this matter.” 



&#x20;



Sally’s comments 



“Due to the fact that this affects one of the smaller production sites, it is not significant to the company’s operations and there is no going concern impact.”



Solution



The following section presents an example of how you should structure your answer. Select the headings





Deterioration of inventory due to inappropriate storage

Consideration of adjusting event 



The vials of vaccine were not stored at an appropriate temperature reducing their efficacy to 10%. The power outage occurred at the end of March impacting inventory planned to be sold in May 20X1. Therefore, this would be considered an adjusting event as the improper storage occurred prior to the year-end so the inventory was damaged prior to the year-end.  



&#x20;



Additional audit evidence required 



Inspect the maintenance logs to confirm the dates of the power outage to confirm that it occurred prior to the year-end 



Confirm from the inventory listing the amount of inventory to be written off and agree to year-end journal postings 



Inspect the maintenance logs and quality control reports to confirm that no other fridges were affected by the power outage which could affect other inventory items



Impact on the audit report 



The value of inventory impaired is £170,000, which is material for audit purposes, even if no other items were affected.  



&#x20;



If the board refuses to write-off the inventory then the issue would be deemed material, but not pervasive as the matter being discussed is confined to a specific item of the financial statements and is unlikely to represent a substantial proportion of the financial statements. 



&#x20;



Type of audit opinion to be issued 



In this situation, we would issue a qualified ‘except for’ opinion.





Administration of client

Consideration of adjusting event



Following the post-year-end payment made by Handler Ltd, an amount of £70,000 remains outstanding but not provided for. As there was evidence of Handler Ltd experiencing financial difficulties at year-end then this would constitute an adjusting event.



However, the amount of £70,000 is not material and therefore would not require an adjustment to the financial statements. This should be reported to management if it is above the trivial threshold and evaluated with any other errors found to be over the trivial threshold.



&#x20;



Additional audit evidence required



Inspect post-year-end bank statements and confirm receipt of payment of £45,000 from Handler Ltd to GP.



Inspect correspondence from the administrator to confirm the balance due to the company as at 30 April 20X1 and any further disbursements likely to be received.



Impact on the audit report/audit opinion



The matter is not material so has no impact on the audit report or audit opinion.





Future production impacted by falsified research results

Consideration of adjusting event



The notification of the issue with the results of the research occurred after the year-end, but the falsification of the results took place prior to the year-end. As such, at the year-end, the project was not viable, and the equipment if unsuitable for any other use by GP, should be impaired resulting in this being an adjusting event. 



&#x20;



Additional audit evidence required



Inspect the communication between GP and Marrow Ltd confirming that the original project will not be going ahead. 



Inspect the communication between GP and Consolidated Medications Ltd for confirmation as to whether the potential project will go ahead and any agreed contracts.



Impact on the audit report 



The value of the equipment is £280,000, which is material for audit purposes and if it cannot be confirmed that the potential project with Consolidated Medications Ltd will be going ahead to utilise the equipment, then the equipment is impaired. 



&#x20;



If the board refuses to write-off the equipment then the issue would be deemed material, but not pervasive as the matter being discussed is confined to a specific item of the financial statements and does not appear to represent a substantial proportion of the financial statements.  



&#x20;



Type of audit opinion to be issued 



In this situation, we would issue a qualified ‘except for’ opinion.





Listing of GP on 1 May 20X1

Consideration of adjusting event 



The actual listing occurred on 1 May 20X1, which is after the year-end, with no conditions in existence at the year-end and as such is a non-adjusting event as at the year-end. However, appropriate disclosure will be required in the financial statements to 30 April 20X1 due to the significance of the event to the company.



Additional audit evidence required 



Inspect the listing brochure for confirmation of their intention to list as at 1 May 20X1.  



Inspect the disclosure included in the financial statements to ensure that it sufficiently describes the listing of the company and the implications for shareholders.



Impact on the audit report 



Although the company does not need to make an adjustment to the figures in the financial statements, the listing of GP is a material event and there should be disclosure in the financial statements explaining that the company is now listed.  



&#x20;



If the listing is disclosed incorrectly then this is likely to be a pervasive matter due to the significance of the event.  



&#x20;



Impact on the audit opinion 



If appropriate disclosure is made, then we will issue an unmodified opinion. Due to the significance of the event, an emphasis of matter paragraph should also be included within the audit report, referencing the disclosure within the financial statements.  



&#x20;



If adequate disclosure is not made, then we will issue an adverse opinion.





Destruction of one of the production facilities due to flooding

Consideration of adjusting event



&#x20;



The flood occurred in early May, after the year-end. There was no indication of there being an issue at the year-end date that would impact the figures in the financial statements, therefore, this would be considered a non-adjusting event.



Additional audit evidence



Enquire with GP as to the status of the flood investigation and inspect any reports available to confirm the value of equipment damaged.  



Inspect a copy of the insurance policy documentation to confirm that the plant and building are covered by the policy. 



Inspect the note in the financial statements confirming that the matter has been adequately disclosed.



Impact on audit report/opinion



If the matter has been disclosed adequately there is no impact on the audit report or audit opinion.  



&#x20;



If the matter is not adequately disclosed then this is likely to be material only as it does not appear to have had a significant impact on company operations. 



If adequate disclosure is not made, then we will issue a qualified ‘except for’ opinion.



#### Question 2



Read the following scenario.



&#x20;



You are the audit manager of Northern Bakers Limited (‘Northern’) for the year ended 31 March 20X0.



&#x20;



Northern is a bakery chain with its head office based in Manchester, which has branches across North-West England. Following completion of the year-end audit fieldwork, you are reviewing the outstanding points noted by your audit senior and information regarding other events provided by Northern’s finance director. Northern’s profit before income taxes for the year was £500,000 and you note from the workpapers that overall materiality is set at £50,000 with a trivial threshold of £2,500.



&#x20;



Outstanding points from audit (from audit senior)  



At 31 March 20X0, Northern was owed £15,500 from a supermarket for goods purchased the previous September which was three months overdue and not included in the allowance for doubtful debts. During the fieldwork performed in June, it was noted that no payments had been received against the balance.  



&#x20;



Northern operates two payrolls: monthly for head office staff and weekly for all bakery staff. During the fieldwork, we obtained sufficient appropriate audit evidence to enable us to audit both the monthly and weekly payroll. However, the head of payroll was on sick leave, and we were unable to obtain evidence to support the year-end bonuses totalling £75,000. From discussions with Northern’s finance director, this is part of a new management reward scheme and bonuses are awarded on the basis of company and personal performance. Each of the five staff members eligible for a bonus has their bonus criteria agreed at their annual review.  



&#x20;



Other events provided by Northern’s finance director  



In May 20X0, the board of directors (‘board’) of Northern engaged an external consultancy firm to examine its environmental, social, and governance (ESG) practices and suggest changes that it could implement, with a particular focus on sustainability and increasing the recycled content of the baked goods packaging. The consultancy report was issued this week and we have added the suggestions into the ESG report within the annual report.  



&#x20;



On 26 February 20X0, a fire broke out within a factory unit where one of Northern’s bakeries is based. The fire started in one of the other companies within the unit and the fire investigation concluded that this was due to an electrical fault in a machine. There was significant damage to the factory unit which was closed for three months for repair. Northern is in the process of suing for loss of earnings due to the other company’s negligence and the loss of production during this time. The lost income claim is for £220,000, but the case is unlikely to be settled until early 20X1. Based on legal advice obtained, the board believes that it is probable that Northern will win the claim and has disclosed the potential gain as a contingent asset within the notes to the financial statements. All costs arising due to the loss of production have been accounted for as they have been incurred. 



&#x20;



Question:



Recommend further substantive procedures, if any, your team will need to perform to obtain additional audit evidence to address these matters and analyse the impact on the audit report and opinion, if any, for each matter in the event that further substantive procedures do not resolve the issue satisfactorily.  





Solution



The following section presents an example of how you should structure your answer.





Outstanding trade receivables balance

The value of the trade receivables could be overstated by £15,500. The amount is individually immaterial, so no further substantive procedures are required.







However, the amount is above the trivial threshold and should be reported to management and evaluated with any other errors found to be over the trivial threshold.







Even if the balance is not recovered, there is no impact on the audit report or audit opinion as the amount is immaterial.





Lack of analysis of bonuses

Contact the head of payroll to request analysis, by individual, of year-end bonuses and a copy of the management reward scheme criteria. 



Inspect the management reward scheme criteria to understand which staff members are eligible for the scheme and how bonuses are calculated. 



For each member of the management reward scheme, recalculate their bonus based on the scheme criteria and compare it to the bonus awarded, investigating any significant differences. 



Due to the size of the bonuses, the inability to obtain sufficient appropriate audit evidence will be material to head office payroll costs but it is unlikely to be pervasive as it is limited to only the office payroll. 



If no further evidence is obtained, we will issue a qualified ‘except for’ opinion due to the inability to obtain sufficient appropriate audit evidence.





Environmental, social, and governance (ESG) report

Obtain Northern’s ESG report and inspect the information contained within it to confirm that it is consistent with our understanding of the ESG practices during the year ended 31 March 20X0 and consistent with the information included within the financial statements.



If the information in the ESG report is misstated for the practices in place at 31 March 20X0 or it is inconsistent with the audited financial information and the matter is not resolved or amended by the board of directors of Northern, there will need to be a description of the inconsistency in the ‘other information’ section of the audit report.



If an inconsistency is detected but this is within the ESG report rather than the financial statements, there is no impact upon the audit opinion.





Contingent asset

Inspect copies of the assumptions and calculations performed to determine the potential compensation of £220,000 and reperform calculations to determine the accuracy of the figure.



Inspect third-party correspondence from Northern’s lawyers and any other available relevant correspondence to determine the likelihood of the case being decided in favour of Northern.



If there is insufficient evidence to support that it is possible that the claim will be awarded in Northern’s favour, and the board refuses to correct then this should not be disclosed as a contingent asset and would represent a material misstatement. In this case, we would issue a qualified ‘except for’ opinion.
