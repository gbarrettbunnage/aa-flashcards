Step 1

When you are asked to analyse risks to data integrity and evaluate their impact on management’s ability to design and implement an effective strategy:

You should begin with identifying risks to data integrity when you are reading the scenario.

You should then analyse the risk by thinking of its implications. You need to think of the problems that risk would cause for data integrity. For example, could it lead to data being lost or changed without authorisation? Or maybe it could affect the accuracy of data?

When asked to evaluate the impact of the risks on the organisation’s strategy, think about the impact of unreliable data on the company’s ability to make proper decisions. For example, would it affect reports that are used for decision making or information that is published for stakeholders or regulatory compliance?

Step 2

When you are asked to analyse information given in the scenario to highlight any concerns over the accuracy, validity and completeness of the underlying data:

You need to begin by identifying the issue from the scenario and then analyse it.

It is important to read the information provided carefully. Look for unusual trends or information that appears to contradict other information and explain them.

Step 3

When you are asked to describe any further audit evidence (or procedures) that are required to conclude on whether reliance can be placed on the underlying data by the audit team:

When explaining evidence (or procedures), ensure that you identify the source document and then go on to explain what you are confirming from the source document.

If you are asked within a question to "conclude" on whether the audit team should place reliance on the data, make sure you include a conclusion in your answer as there will be marks in the solution allocated to this.

Question 1

Scenario

You are the audit senior on the audit of Rainbow Sausages Ltd (Rainbow), a manufacturer of premium frozen vegan sausages. This is the second year of trading.

The company has a year end of 31 March 20X1 and it is now 24 January 20X1. You are considering the range of procedures required in order to audit inventory, which comprises raw materials and finished products. Work in progress is immaterial.

Rainbow manufactures four ranges of sausage, named after colours of the rainbow (Red, Orange, Yellow and Green) and has a factory just outside of Perth, Scotland. Additional natural colourings are added to the sausages in order to make them attractive to children.

Raw materials and finished products are stored in separate warehouses. The company maintains a perpetual inventory counting system, with all product lines counted at least once a year, and there are no plans to conduct a full count at the year end. Your firm sent an audit assistant to attend one of the counts on 31 July 20X0.

Your audit firm plans to utilise audit data analytics (ADA) as part of its audit approach for inventory. This will include routines over audit risk analysis as well as substantive testing. Your firm’s internal analytics team, who are not otherwise involved in the audit process, have extracted inventory data from the client’s records. The extracted data has been cleansed and uploaded to your audit firm’s ADA software, with no issues noted. Several outputs have been produced (Appendix) and your audit manager has also annotated some relevant notes following the planning meeting with Rainbow’s financial controller.

To place reliance on the inventory data for the purposes of the audit, your manager has asked you to analyse the outputs to confirm if there are any concerns over the completeness, accuracy and validity of the data gathered relating to inventory. She would also like you to describe, if applicable, any further audit evidence that may be required to conclude on whether reliance can be placed on the underlying data by the audit team.

Questions:

Analyse the results of the outputs of the audit data analytic exercise over inventory to highlight any concerns over the accuracy, validity and completeness of the underlying data related to inventory; and

Solution

The following section presents an example of how you should structure your answer.

Inventory days

Inventory days of Orange in August were 389. This is not in line with the usual trend for this product and may indicate that inventory days data is inaccurate for August.

Inventory days of Green are negative in December. This is indicative of either the stock value or cost of sales figures being negative, which indicates that this data is likely inaccurate.

Further evidence required:

We should obtain and inspect the underlying calculations for inventory days and agree these back to the accounting system to confirm that underlying data is accurate and how a negative figure has been produced.

Results of inventory counts

It is noted that, for each of the counts conducted since the last year end, there have been shortfalls noted. This may suggest that the overall inventory on the ledger is not valid and not reflective of actual inventory held. However, all quantities counted are round sum amounts, which appears unusual and may indicate that inventory quantities counted are inaccurate.

In May 20X0, some products were stored offsite following a flood. It is possible that this may account for the shortfall on Red (if counters did not visit the offsite storage in Blairgowrie).

Further evidence required regarding results of inventory counts:

Inventory count instructions should be obtained and inspected, along with inventory count sheets to establish whether individual items were accurately counted.

We should inspect the test counts performed by the audit assistant and compare items to confirm that all items were round sum amounts.

Enquire with management whether offsite stock was included in the stock count and ask for records of stock count sheets for those items.

Enquire with management about the reason for the discrepancies noted.

Gross margin data

The gross profit margin achieved on Red is 23% above both the margin in the prior year and in the budget. The increase in margin appears unusual given the recall of products in December 20X0, which would require to be written off. Therefore, this may indicate that the gross margin data for Red is inaccurate.

Further evidence required regarding Red’s gross margin data:

We should confirm how the recalled products have been accounted for in financial statements and whether they are included in the calculation.

The data used for the gross margin calculation should be inspected and agreed to the accounting system.

The gross profit margin reported on Yellow is 18% negative. This is inconsistent with the data on cost (£2.50) and net realisable value (NRV) (£2.70), which would indicate that sales of Yellow should be profitable. This may indicate that gross margin data for Yellow is inaccurate.

Further evidence required regarding Yellow's gross margin data:

Confirm with management whether there have been any changes in NRV of Yellow and agree these to recent sales invoices.

Question 2

Scenario

Read the following scenario:

It is April 20X1 and you are the audit senior for the audit of Wax Ltd (Wax), a firm of chartered surveyors, for the year ended 31 March 20X1. The audit is currently at the planning stage, and you are considering the required audit procedures to gain assurance over Wax's plant, property and equipment (PPE) balance as at 31 March 20X1.

Wax is a long-established firm based in Manchester which specialises in commercial and residential surveying. PPE includes office equipment that is used by staff working both in offices and at client sites, a number of vehicles, office equipment such as desks and chairs, the company's head office building in Manchester and relevant fixtures and fittings. Wax uses an Excel spreadsheet as an asset register to record all transactions in relation to PPE.

This year’s audit strategy will include audit data analytics (ADA) as part of testing procedures for this account balance in the financial statements.

Your audit firm’s policy when using ADA requires your firm's IT audit department to extract data from a client’s system using the firm’s in-house data extraction tool. The IT audit department has therefore extracted data from the client's PPE asset register, performed relevant cleansing procedures over the extracted data and uploaded it into the firm's ADA software.

The ADA software will be used to run specific routines over the depreciation charge and average carrying values of non-current assets in order to confirm the accuracy, valuation and allocation of the depreciation charge in the financial statements and to assess the appropriateness of Wax’s depreciation policy.

Depreciation has been identified as an area for focus due to information provided by Wax’s financial controller during the audit planning meeting. A number of staff vehicles had been traded in during November 20X0 at a loss compared to the carrying value per the PPE asset register. All disposals had been subject to trade-in at market value set by the company’s main supplier of motor vehicles and the accounting losses arising suggested that depreciation charges had not been allocated appropriately or that there was an issue with Wax’s current depreciation policy.

In order to ensure that the outputs from the ADA software are appropriate for use in the audit, your audit manager has asked you to undertake procedures to confirm the accuracy, validity and completeness of the data gathered relating to PPE and to conclude on whether the data is appropriate for use by the audit team. Wax’s financial controller has provided you with extracts from Wax’s accounting system and ledger as well as supporting information in relation to transactions recorded to PPE additions and disposals with net book value (NBV) greater than £7,200. Your audit partner has agreed this threshold for testing individual transactions.

Question:

Apply the procedures that the audit team should perform to confirm the accuracy, validity and completeness of the information provided for PPE. Document and evaluate the results of your procedures, including any issues identified with the data, and conclude on whether the data is appropriate for use by the audit team.

Section C – Extract from Wax Ltd asset capitalisation and depreciation manual

All relevant assets are to be capitalised in line with accounting standards at cost. Relevant assets include new PPE additions, but not costs associated with repairs or general maintenance. All assets are allocated a sequential asset number based on their asset class in order of date of acquisition.

In line with tax legislation, the capitalised cost of motor vehicles includes VAT as this cannot be reclaimed.

Depreciation is charged in the year of purchase, but no depreciation is charged in the year of disposal. The standard depreciation policies/estimated useful lives for company assets are:

Buildings – 10% diminishing balance

Fixtures and fittings – 10% diminishing balance

Office equipment – 5 - 10 years straight line

Motor vehicles – 5 - 10 years straight line

Comment:

Motor vehicle GJ70 FTW has not been capitalised including VAT, which is not in line with the company’s capitalisation manual and indicates the data is inaccurate.

However, the date of purchase per the invoice is out with the current financial year, which would indicate that the asset should not be recorded as an addition within the asset register as at 31 March 20X1. The asset may have been received prior to issue of the invoice, else the data is invalid.

Solution

Select each heading to review the solution.

Data extracted from the ledger

The total PPE cost brought forward and accumulated depreciation per the accounting system of £989,403 and £492,313, respectively, agrees to the summary totals per the PPE asset register (asset register).

The total additions per the extract from the asset register agree to the summary total. However, the total additions of £51,199 per the asset register do not agree to the accounting system additions of £42,650. The difference of £8,549 may relate to an addition (motor vehicle - GJ70 FTW #35C), posted to the asset register on the last day of the financial period but which has not been posted to the accounting system – in error – resulting in the accounting system being incomplete.

Furthermore, the number of items included in the accounting system is 14, but there are 15 items in the register, which further supports a potential omission.

Sequencing of asset additions

The sequencing of numbers allocated to motor vehicles does not identify any potential omissions. However, the sequence of numbers allotted to office equipment assets contains gaps from asset number #94B to #97B and #103B to #105B. This may indicate that items of office equipment have been omitted from the asset register and that additions are incomplete.

Non-capital expenditure

Costs associated with painting and servicing of cars of £450 and £7,452 have been recorded in the asset register which are not in line with the company’s capitalisation policy and would indicate the inclusion of invalid additions

Additions – invoices

The amounts included in the asset register for the two purchased motor vehicles, JK69 WJI and FJ69 FIT, agree to the amounts per the purchase invoices and have been capitalised including VAT, in line with the company’s capitalisation manual.

Motor vehicle GJ70 FTW has not been capitalised including VAT, which is not in line with the company’s capitalisation manual and indicates the data is inaccurate.

However, the date of purchase per the invoice is out with the current financial year, which would indicate that the asset should not be recorded as an addition within the asset register as at 31 March 20X1. The asset may have been received prior to issue of the invoice, otherwise the data is invalid.

Disposals

The disposals per the asset register summary of £3,500 agree to the trade-in invoice of 18/11/20X0. However, it is unlikely that the carrying amount of the asset disposed of agrees exactly to the disposal value (particularly given it has been highlighted that accounting losses have been incurred on disposal of motor vehicles in the year) and, therefore, there is a risk the asset register summary value of £3,500 is inaccurate.

Furthermore, it was noted during planning discussions that the company had disposed of a number of motor vehicles and, therefore, only one disposal is not in line with our understanding of the business and may indicate data relating to disposals is incomplete.

Conclusion

Due to the issues identified in relation to the data population, the auditor should not currently place reliance on the data extracted from the system.
